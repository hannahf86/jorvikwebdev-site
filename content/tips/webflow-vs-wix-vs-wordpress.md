<p>Full disclosure: we build in Webflow. So take this with appropriate salt. But we've worked in all three, and here's what's actually true.</p><h2>WordPress</h2><p>WordPress powers about 43% of the internet. It's free, hugely flexible, and has a plugin for literally everything. It's also the most hacked CMS on the planet, requires regular maintenance, and can become a slow, bloated mess if you're not careful about what plugins you install.</p><p><strong>Best for:</strong> Blogs, content-heavy sites, people who want total control and don't mind the maintenance overhead. Also good if you need very specific functionality that isn't available elsewhere.</p><h2>Wix</h2><p>Wix is the easiest to use of the three. Drag, drop, done. The trade-off is you're quite limited in what you can customise, the code it generates isn't always clean, and it can get expensive once you need proper features.</p><p><strong>Best for:</strong> Small local businesses who want something up quickly and won't need much customisation. Great for getting started.</p><h2>Webflow</h2><p>Webflow gives designers and developers the most control without writing much code. The CMS is genuinely excellent. It's more expensive than the others but the quality of what you can produce — and the speed at which it loads — is notably better.</p><p><strong>Best for:</strong> Businesses who want a professional, fast, well-designed website that's easy to hand over to a client and easy to maintain. Which is why we use it.</p>
