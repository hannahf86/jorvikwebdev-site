<p>KPI stands for Key Performance Indicator. The word "key" is doing a lot of work there, because most small businesses track either everything or nothing. Neither is useful.</p><h2>Start with your goal</h2><p>Before you pick a metric, decide what you want your website to do. If it's supposed to get people to book a call, track bookings. If it's supposed to sell products, track sales and conversion rate. The metric follows the goal, not the other way round.</p><h2>The ones worth tracking for most small businesses</h2><p><strong>Sessions</strong> — how many people visited your site. Baseline number. Useful for spotting trends over time.</p><p><strong>Bounce rate</strong> — the percentage of people who left without clicking anything. High bounce rate on your homepage usually means your message isn't landing fast enough.</p><p><strong>Conversion rate</strong> — the percentage of visitors who did the thing you wanted (booked, bought, enquired). This is the number that matters most.</p><p><strong>Traffic source</strong> — where are people coming from? Google, social media, direct? Tells you what's working and where to put your energy.</p><p><strong>Top pages</strong> — which pages get the most traffic? Often surprising. Could reveal content gaps or unexpected strengths.</p><h2>What not to obsess over</h2><p>Follower counts. Likes. Time on page (usually). Vanity metrics feel good and tell you very little. A page with low time-on-page might just mean people found their answer fast and went to book.</p>
