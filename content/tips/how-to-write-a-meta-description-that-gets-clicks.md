<p>A meta description is the two lines of text that appear under your page title in Google search results. It doesn't directly affect your ranking, but it massively affects whether someone clicks on your result or the one below it.</p><h2>The formula</h2><p>Here's a structure that works for almost any page:</p><p><strong>[What the page is about] + [Who it's for] + [What they'll get or be able to do]</strong></p><p>Example for a services page: "Web design for small businesses in York. We build fast, eco-friendly Webflow sites that turn visitors into customers — and we teach you how to run them."</p><p>That's 156 characters. It says what it is, who it's for, and what you get. Done.</p><h2>The rules</h2><p>Keep it under 160 characters or Google will cut it off mid-sentence, which looks bad. Include your main keyword naturally — don't stuff it in awkwardly. Write for the human reading it, not for Google. Use active language. "Get a free quote" beats "Free quotes are available."</p><h2>The mistakes</h2><p>Leaving it blank (Google will just grab random text from your page — usually something unhelpful). Copying it from another page. Making it too vague ("We offer many great services for businesses of all sizes" tells nobody anything). Keyword stuffing ("web design web designer website design York Yorkshire" is not a sentence).</p><h2>One more thing</h2><p>Every page needs its own unique meta description. Yes, all of them. No, your homepage description does not work for your contact page.</p>
