<p>A website brief is the document you hand to a web designer before they start work. Most briefs are either three words long ("something clean and professional") or a 40-page PDF that answers none of the questions a designer actually needs answered.</p><h2>What a designer actually needs to know</h2><p><strong>What does your business do?</strong> Not your mission statement. The actual thing. "We make handmade candles and sell them online and at markets."</p><p><strong>Who is your ideal customer?</strong> Be specific. "Women aged 30-50 who care about sustainability and buy gifts for other people" is useful. "Everyone" is not.</p><p><strong>What do you want visitors to do?</strong> Buy something? Book a call? Fill in a form? There should be one primary action. Maybe two. Not seven.</p><p><strong>What do you like and dislike?</strong> Share 3-5 websites you like and explain what you like about them. Share some you hate and say why. This saves hours of misaligned design directions.</p><p><strong>What's your timeline and budget?</strong> Yes, you need to say the number. "We have a budget" is not a budget. Designers use this to scope what's possible.</p><h2>What you don't need in a brief</h2><p>You don't need to specify fonts, exact colours, or specific layouts unless you're a designer yourself. That's their job. Give them the context; let them do the creative thinking.</p>
